# Bacotti Enterprises — Editorial Standards
## Across All Publications: Bithues, Succession, Dependability

---

## The Rule

**No bias political commentary. No woke ideology. No climate change advocacy. No social justice framing.**

This applies to every piece of content published under every byline on every domain. Not as a negotiation — as a condition of publication.

---

## What This Means in Practice

### Political Neutrality
We do not editorialize about political parties, political figures, elections, or political movements. We do not attribute economic or market outcomes to political ideologies. We do not use language that carries political valance — charged terms that signal agreement or disagreement with political positions rather than describing facts.

This means we don't write things like:
- "The administration's policy caused a market downturn" (political attribution)
- "Investors are fleeing blue-state markets" (political framing)
- "The Republican tax cuts benefited real estate investors" (political framing)
- "ESG-focused funds are outperforming" (political framing embedded in investment analysis)

Instead, we write: "The S&P 500 fell 1.2% following the Fed's announcement of a 50-basis-point rate increase. Historical data on rate increases during periods of elevated inflation suggests this is within the range of typical market responses to monetary policy adjustments."

### No Woke / No Social Justice
We don't cover diversity, equity, inclusion, structural racism, gender identity, or social justice as topics in our publications. This isn't because these topics are unimportant — it's because they are outside the scope of what we publish. Bithues covers books and literature. Succession covers real estate education. Dependability covers financial research. None of these publications are platforms for social or political advocacy.

This means we don't write things like:
- "The housing market reflects systemic inequities in wealth distribution"
- "This novel explores themes of racial identity and social marginalization through its protagonist" (in a review that uses this as the main lens rather than literary analysis)
- "Investors are increasingly focused on DEI metrics in their portfolio decisions" (as a positive or normative statement)

Where a book or topic genuinely touches on these areas as part of its content, we can describe that content factually — "the novel depicts a Black family navigating gentrification in Chicago" — without endorsing a social or political interpretation of it.

### No Climate Content
We don't cover climate change, climate policy, ESG investing, or environmental topics in our financial or real estate publications. This applies specifically to Dependability and Succession. Bithues may cover books that engage with environmental themes, but we don't editorialize about climate as a topic and we don't frame investments through environmental lenses.

### No Identity-Based Commentary
We don't analyze markets, industries, or cultural trends through the lens of racial, gender, sexual, or other identity-based frameworks. We don't write about "women investors," "Latino homebuyers," or "the Black middle class" as statistical categories in ways that imply political commentary. When demographic data is relevant to a topic, we cite it factually without political framing.

---

## Why These Standards Exist

These publications are built to be useful reference resources, not political platforms. Our readers come to us for analysis, not for commentary on culture wars. That usefulness — and the trust it builds — is what makes the publications valuable.

This isn't about pretending the world doesn't have political dimensions. It's about staying in our lane so that the content we produce remains reliable and relevant regardless of who is in power, what the current culture war is, or which direction the wind is blowing.

When we stick to this standard, our content ages well. An article from five years ago that says "the Fed raised rates by 25 basis points and the market responded with X" is still accurate and useful. An article that says "this proves the Fed is bowing to political pressure from [political faction]" is embarrassing and wrong in ways that have nothing to do with the data.

## Exceptions and Gray Areas

**Books that engage with political themes:** Bithues reviews books as literary works. A novel about immigration, a memoir about activism, or a non-fiction book about housing policy can be reviewed honestly. The review describes what the book says and evaluates how well it says it — not whether the book's politics align with the reviewer's views or with any particular agenda.

**Real estate and climate risk:** Succession can mention climate risk as a factual factor in property valuation (flood zone designation, wildfire risk, etc.) without making climate policy arguments. "This property is in Flood Zone A per FEMA's Flood Map Service Center" is factual. "Climate policy is destroying property values in coastal markets" is not.

**Financial markets and political events:** Dependability can describe the market effects of political events factually — "Markets fell following the unexpected result of the debt ceiling vote" — without attributing moral judgments to the event or suggesting political conclusions.

The test is: **are we describing facts or advocating positions?** Description is fine. Advocacy is not.
